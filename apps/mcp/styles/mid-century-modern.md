## Tone
Cheerful, sociable and well-made: a 1960s architect's home that still feels fresh.

## Principles
- Wood is the hero: one dominant teak or walnut tone, repeated in legs, sideboard and frames.
- Limit colour to two or three accents (mustard, teal, burnt orange), used in textiles and ceramics.
- Pieces stand on slim tapered legs so the floor shows through.
- Mix organic curves with straight lines.
- A sculptural lamp is a must in each main room.

## Séjour / cuisine
Cream walls, optionally the sofa wall in teal. A low sofa with tapered legs, an oval wood coffee table, an arc floor lamp, a leather or tweed lounge chair. A long sideboard as TV unit. Round wood dining table with four chairs under a globe pendant. Keep the kitchen tiles; add a wood tray and ceramics.

## Chambres
Walnut bed on tapered legs, two matching nightstands with ceramic lamps, mustard or burnt orange cushions, cream curtains. Second room: a teak desk and chair, a shelf with objects.

## Entrée
A small sideboard or wall shelf with a round mirror and a ceramic dish; wooden hooks.

## Salle de bain
Keep tiles and fittings. Teal towels, a wooden stool, a round mirror, a plant.

## WC
A small abstract print, a plant, one coloured towel.

## Balcon
A teak-look folding set, a ceramic planter, a mustard cushion.

## Do
- Repeat the wood tone in at least four pieces.
- Show legs: keep storage off the floor when possible.
- Place a colourful ceramic on every sideboard.

## Don't
- No chrome tubes and glass, no gloss white.
- Don't use more than three accent colours.
- No faux-antique ornament.
